Why a visible meeting timer makes meetings better

Meetings expand to fill the time available — and then some. A countdown everyone can see creates light, shared time pressure that keeps discussion focused and signals when it's time to move on. Time-boxing each agenda item is one of the simplest, most effective ways to make meetings shorter without making them feel rushed.

How to use it

  • Give each agenda item its own time box (our agenda builder assigns minutes per item).
  • Share your screen so the whole room — including remote attendees — sees the clock.
  • When it hits zero, explicitly decide: move on, or agree to extend. Don't drift.
  • Use the 25-minute preset for a focused "meeting sprint" with a hard stop.
  • For a quick round of introductions or a lightning update, the 1-minute and 3-minute presets give each person the same slice.

Common meeting timer settings

Most meeting time boxes fall into a handful of lengths. Tap a preset, or type any number of minutes into the custom box — it accepts anything from 1 to 240.

LengthWhat it's good for
60 secondsOne-minute updates in a standup, timed introductions, a round where everyone gets the same airtime.
3 minutesLightning talks, a per-person check-in, a strictly bounded tangent.
5–10 minutesA single agenda item, a demo, or a focused discussion before a decision.
15 minutesA daily standup, or a short one-to-one. See how to run a standup.
25 minutesA "meeting sprint" with a hard stop — and five minutes back before the next call.
45–60 minutesWorkshops and longer working sessions, usually split into smaller timed blocks.

Using it as a countdown to meeting start — or a break clock

The timer isn't only for agenda items. Two of the most useful ways to run it have nothing to do with the discussion itself:

  • Countdown to meeting start. Share your screen a few minutes early with the clock running down. Late joiners can see exactly how long they have, and the meeting starts on time without anyone having to police it.
  • Break clock. In a long workshop, put a 10-minute countdown on the shared screen when you break. "Back in ten" is vague; a visible clock is not, and people return on time far more reliably.

Tap Meeting starts soon or 10-min break to set the time and the message in one go, or type your own message — it appears above the clock in large text, including in full screen. To count down to a fixed start time instead of a duration, pick a clock time in the count down to box. Copy link to this timer gives you a URL with the time and message built in, so you can drop it in the chat or bookmark your usual setup.

In a meeting room, cast the full-screen view to the room display so it works as a room timer everyone can glance at. On a video call, share the browser tab so remote attendees see the same clock as the people in the room — one of the small fairness fixes in our hybrid meetings guide.
