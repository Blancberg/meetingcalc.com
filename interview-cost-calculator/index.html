What goes into the cost of an interview

The headline cost most people miss is interviewer time. Every interview bills the salary of everyone on the panel for its full length — plus, in reality, the prep and debrief around it. The cost per interview is length (hours) × interviewers × loaded hourly rate; multiply by interviews per hire to get the cost of filling one role, and by hires per year for the annual total.

This calculator counts interviewer salary time only. A full cost-per-hire also includes recruiter time, sourcing tools, job ads, and referral bonuses — but interviewer time is usually the largest and most overlooked piece.

How to reduce interview cost without lowering the bar

  • Cut redundant rounds — more interviews rarely means better hiring decisions past a point.
  • Use a structured scorecard so panels gather signal efficiently instead of re-covering ground.
  • Right-size the panel; two focused interviewers often beat four unfocused ones.
  • Screen earlier so only strong candidates reach expensive late-stage panels.
